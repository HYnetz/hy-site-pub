Overview As climate change, COVID-19, and conflict continue to escalate globally, building inclusive, cohesive, and resilient communities will be the foundation of making and protecting sustainable development gains. However, the reality is that too often persons with disabilities are left out of conversations around these critical issues, even though they are among some of the most affected groups.Without a disabilityinclusive approach to recovery and building resilience, persons with disabilities are at increased risk of being further left behind and falling further into poverty due to overlapping crises. The International Day of Persons with Disabilities is a day for championing the rights of persons with disabilities and to increase awareness of the challenges individuals face globally. This international day is observed each year on December 3. It has two primary goals: first, to promote the full and equal participation of persons with disabilities; and second, to ensure the inclusion of persons with disabilities in all aspects of society and development. Thursday, December 2 Breaking Barriers: Disability Inclusion in Latin America and the Caribbean 11:00AM - 12PM EST About 85 million persons with disabilities live in Latin America and the Caribbean. Their exclusion is unfair and unsustainable: 15% of children with disabilities do not go to school and 1 in 2 persons with disabilities do not participate in the labor market.
