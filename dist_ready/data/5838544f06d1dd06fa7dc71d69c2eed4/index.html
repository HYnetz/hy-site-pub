The World Bank Group (WBG) has launched public consultations to update its 2015 Systematic Country Diagnostic (SCD) for Bangladesh as a first step to prepare the new Country Partnership Framework (CPF) for FY 2023-27 that will outline the WBG’s strategic support to the country for the next five years. SCD identifies key challenges and opportunities for a country to achieve its growth aspirations. The 2021 SCD will be an update of the previous SCD, taking into account the new and pressing challenges since 2015, including the unprecedented health and economic impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. For this, the World Bank is seeking your and other stakeholders' views on Bangladesh’s development priorities. Please take the survey in English or in Bangla today! MULTIMEDIA click Overview and Process Priorities identified in 2015 SCD and beyond The first SCD for Bangladesh was conducted in 2015, identified job creation as the key development priority for the country. It identified five transformational priorities: energy, inland connectivity, regional and global integration, urbanization, and adaptive delta management. It also highlighted three foundational priorities—macroeconomic stability, human development, and institutional and business environment—where Bangladesh was doing fairly well and would benefit from continued strong performance in these areas. Based on bottlenecks identified in the previous SCD and new issues since 2015, the infographic (see below) shows the broad categories of development priorities for Bangladesh for the next five to ten years. Please check this list of possible bottlenecks and, in your opinion, what are the top 3 priorities for Bangladesh to reduce poverty and boost shared prosperity in the next 5-10 years? Please send your feedback by June 21, 2021. SCD Consultation Process and Schedule To update the SCD, the World Bank Group will conduct focus group discussions and online consultations in two rounds with a wide range of stakeholders, including government, private sector, think tanks, civil society organizations, youth, development partners, media, academia, and think tanks. The World Group launched the first round of consultations. Due to the COVID-19 pandemic, all consultations sessions were held virtually.
